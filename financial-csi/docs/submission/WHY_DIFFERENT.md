# Why This Is Different

**Why isn’t this just fraud detection?**
Fraud detection usually targets bad actors (stolen cards, IP anomalies). Financial CSI targets systemic interactions (timeouts, retries, webhook latencies) that cause operational loss.

**Why isn’t this just anomaly detection?**
Standard anomaly detection flags "unusual" behavior, which generates massive noise. Our Apriori engine requires a statistically significant correlation with *actual financial loss* on a held-out test set before surfacing a pattern.

**Why isn’t this just an LLM chatbot?**
LLMs hallucinate financial metrics. We restrict the AI exclusively to interpreting deterministic `EvidencePacks` generated by the backend. It does not discover fraud; it merely translates mathematical discoveries into forensic briefs for humans.

**Why isn’t this just a dashboard?**
Dashboards look backward at what happened. This system discovers the pattern, proves it generalizes, and mathematically simulates the future savings of fixing it.

**Why isn’t this just a rules engine?**
Rules engines rely on human analysts predicting the exact failure conditions upfront. Financial CSI mines "Emergent Patterns" directly from the noise of the data—patterns that were not explicitly encoded into our baseline rules.

**Why does the counterfactual simulator matter?**
Blocking 100% of suspicious traffic stops loss, but it bankrupts the merchant by blocking legitimate customers. The simulator calculates whether the intervention actually saves money.

**Why does false-positive cost matter?**
An intervention (like enforcing 3DS or delaying capture) has friction. If the friction costs more than the prevented loss, the system actively refuses to intervene.

**Why does event-level evidence matter?**
A merchant cannot act on a black-box "Risk Score: 92." They need to see the exact sequence of events (e.g., *Timeout → Rapid Retry → Delayed Webhook*) to fix the underlying integration or operational gap.
